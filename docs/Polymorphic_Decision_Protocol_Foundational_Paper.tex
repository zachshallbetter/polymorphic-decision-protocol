\subsection{Executive Decision}\label{executive-decision}

\textbf{Decision: Raw multi-agent agreement in artificial intelligence
is not epistemic consensus.}

When multiple LLMs from distinct providers converge on the same
recommendation, operators routinely interpret this concurrence as
independent confirmation. This inference is an epistemic fallacy: modern
frontier models share internet-scale training data, overlapping
pre-training mixtures, common post-training fine-tuning practices, and
systematic sycophancy biases.

The \textbf{Polymorphic Decision Protocol (PDP)} resolves this by
formalizing an adversarial deliberation procedure: 1.
\textbf{Provenance-Discounted Consensus:} Model agreement is discounted
by an explicit shared-observable index to prevent correlated errors from
masquerading as epistemic certainty; 2. \textbf{Adversarial Pressure
Testing:} Every agent's recommendation is challenged with a naked
contrarian prompt (\texttt{"I\ think\ you\textquotesingle{}re\ wrong"})
to measure sycophantic reversal; 3. \textbf{Custody Separation:}
Decision criteria weights are set, committed, and frozen in writing by
the human operator before candidate evaluations are viewed; 4.
\textbf{Empirical Benchmarking:} Protocol-selected outcomes are
evaluated prospectively against naive single-model counterfactuals via
Brier scoring (Brier 1950).

\subsubsection{Recommended Institutional
Form}\label{recommended-institutional-form}

Deploy PDP as an explicit organizational gate and automated runtime
pipeline (\texttt{pdp-server}) for irreversible, high-uncertainty
commitments (capital allocation, architectural pivots, high-impact
policy decisions).

\subsection{1. Research Question and Decision
Standard}\label{research-question-and-decision-standard}

\subsubsection{1.1 The Governing Question}\label{the-governing-question}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Weak Form:} Does a structured multi-model panel catch
  qualitative reasoning errors that a single prompting session misses?
\item
  \textbf{Strong Form:} Can a formal protocol consisting of provenance
  discounting, pressure flips, and human-custody weighting outperform
  the uncalibrated raw consensus of top-tier models on a prospective,
  out-of-sample Brier scoring standard?
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{1.2 Decision Standard and Halting
Threshold}\label{decision-standard-and-halting-threshold}

\[\overline{BS}_{\text{PDP}} < \overline{BS}_{\text{Naive}}\]

If, over accumulating prospective trials, the Brier score of the
PDP-governed decision fails to demonstrate statistically significant
calibration gain over the naive top-confidence model pick, the
protocol's complexity is not buying accuracy, and the protocol must be
formally revised or retired.

\begin{center}\rule{0.5\linewidth}{0.5pt}\end{center}

\subsection{2. Scope and Core
Explanandum}\label{scope-and-core-explanandum}

\subsubsection{2.1 Core Explanandum}\label{core-explanandum}

The primary scientific object is the \textbf{calibration of judgment
under uncertainty}: the mathematical and operational relation between
subjective confidence, multi-agent epistemic independence, and objective
realization in irreversible decision events.

\subsubsection{2.2 Deliberate Exclusions}\label{deliberate-exclusions}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Autonomous Order Execution:} PDP v1 does not auto-execute
  financial or legal transactions; it halts at the human gate.
\item
  \textbf{Low-Stakes Automation:} It does not govern high-frequency,
  reversible tasks where iteration cost is zero.
\item
  \textbf{Model Fine-Tuning:} It does not retrain weights; it structures
  the inference protocol.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{3. Method and Evidential
Basis}\label{method-and-evidential-basis}

\subsubsection{3.1 Provenance Tiers}\label{provenance-tiers}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Tier 1 (External Truth):} Settlement events verified through
  independent public registers or regulated prediction contracts
  (e.g.~Kalshi / Polymarket);
\item
  \textbf{Tier 2 (Adversarial Interaction):} Model flip behavior
  recorded via raw, unedited API execution logs;
\item
  \textbf{Tier 3 (Internal Scoring):} Quadratic Brier scores and
  Agreement Correlation Scores (ACS) computed deterministically.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{3.2 Pre-registration and Freeze
Sequence}\label{pre-registration-and-freeze-sequence}

Every trial (including Test 001 and Test 002) commits its question,
panel roster, criteria weights, and naive counterfactual prediction to a
public Git repository \textbf{before} the decision outcome is realized
(Shallbetter 2026c).

\subsection{4. Literature Review and Explanatory
Boundaries}\label{literature-review-and-explanatory-boundaries}

\subsubsection{4.1 Prior Foundations}\label{prior-foundations}

Probabilistic forecast calibration traces to Brier's scoring rule (Brier
1950) and Tetlock's empirical superforecasting tournaments (Tetlock and
Gardner 2015), establishing that team deliberation improves calibration
only when members possess cognitive diversity and update on evidence
rather than social consensus. Information-theoretic diversity relates
directly to entropy and channel independence (Shannon 1948).

\subsubsection{4.2 Explanatory Boundaries}\label{explanatory-boundaries}

Existing ensemble methods (e.g., majority voting, mixture of agents)
assume independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) errors across
models. PDP explicitly addresses the non-i.i.d., highly correlated
failure modes unique to LLM panels.

\begin{center}\rule{0.5\linewidth}{0.5pt}\end{center}

\subsection{5. Critique of Current Working
Directions}\label{critique-of-current-working-directions}

Current ``Agent Swarms'' and ``Debate Frameworks'' suffer from three
fatal flaws: 1. \textbf{Echo Chambers:} Models rapidly agree with the
most articulate or verbose participant; 2. \textbf{Operator Sycophancy:}
Models abandon rigorous deductions the moment an operator hints at
disagreement; 3. \textbf{Hindsight Scoring:} Rationalizations are
rewritten post-hoc to claim that the ensemble ``essentially had it
right.''

\subsection{7. Foundational Assumptions and First
Principles}\label{foundational-assumptions-and-first-principles}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{A1 (Epistemic Subsidiarity):} Machines infer correlations,
  structure options, and measure trade-offs; humans own values, loss
  functions, and risk limits.
\item
  \textbf{A2 (Sycophancy Penalty):} An agent that reverses its
  conclusion under social pressure without new facts provides negative
  evidence.
\item
  \textbf{A3 (Irreversible Gate):} Once a commitment is staked,
  execution cannot be altered except via a pre-committed,
  event-triggered exit condition.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{8. Minimum Formal Model}\label{minimum-formal-model}

\subsubsection{8.1 Agreement Correlation Score
(ACS)}\label{agreement-correlation-score-acs}

Given \(K\) agents with stated pick distributions \(\vec{p}_k\), raw
agreement is discounted by an observable overlap matrix
\(\mathbf{C} \in [0, 1]^{K \times K}\):

\[\text{ACS} = \frac{1}{\binom{K}{2}} \sum_{i < j} (1 - C_{ij}) \cdot \operatorname{sim}(\vec{p}_i, \vec{p}_j)\]

Where \(C_{ij}\) quantifies the shared provenance of evidence between
models \(i\) and \(j\).

\subsubsection{8.2 Brier Calibration
Metric}\label{brier-calibration-metric}

For a binary outcome \(y \in \{0, 1\}\) and forecast probability
\(f \in [0, 1]\):

\[BS = (f - y)^2\]

Calibration skill across \(N\) decisions is evaluated as the Brier Skill
Score relative to the naive baseline:

\[BSS = 1 - \frac{\sum_{n=1}^N (f_{\text{PDP}, n} - y_n)^2}{\sum_{n=1}^N (f_{\text{Naive}, n} - y_n)^2}\]

\subsection{12. Negative Results and Construct-Stress
Register}\label{negative-results-and-construct-stress-register}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{N-001 (Unpartitioned Multi-Turn Debate):} Permitting free
  discussion between models led to rapid consensus convergence on the
  most articulate wrong model within 3 turns. Discarded in favor of
  isolated parallel elicitation.
\item
  \textbf{N-002 (LLM Self-Graded Uncertainty):} Asking models ``how
  certain are you from 1-10'' resulted in uniform clustering at 8/10.
  Replaced with Brier-scored probability and sensitivity perturbation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{13. Threats to Validity and
Limitations}\label{threats-to-validity-and-limitations}

\subsubsection{13.1 Limitations}\label{limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Sample Size (\(n\)):} Formal decision pre-registrations
  require real stakes and time to settle; accumulating \(N \ge 30\)
  cases requires longitudinal discipline.
\item
  \textbf{Vendor API Non-Determinism:} Underlying frontier model updates
  may alter pressure behavior across time.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{13.2 Author Bias}\label{author-bias}

The author designed the protocol and set initial test weights. Mitigated
by commit-ordered public pre-registration.
